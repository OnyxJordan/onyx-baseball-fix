The Origin

Why the NBA model starts with minutes

Baseball props live and die on one plate appearance. Basketball props are different: a player's points, rebounds and assists are mostly a function of how long he is on the floor, how much of the offense runs through him while he is there, and how many possessions the game has. Get those three right and the stat line follows.

So the model is built as a chain: minutes × production per minute × the game environment, then turned into an over or under probability and checked against the Onyx line. Every link was replayed over the full 2025-26 season, one night at a time, before it was allowed to ship.

Pace & Possessions

Tonight's game environment

Possessions per game, offense and defense per 100 possessions, and net rating for every team on the slate. Game poss is what the model expects tonight: a fast team facing a slow one lands in between (team pace × opponent pace ÷ league pace). Ratings are regressed toward league average until a team has played about ten games, so early-season numbers stay sane.

Usage & Utilization

Who the offense runs through tonight

Usage rate is the share of his team's plays a player finishes while he is on the floor: shots, trips to the line and turnovers (FGA + 0.44 FTA + TOV per minute, scaled to a 48-minute team game). Utilization is his projected share of the game's minutes. +/- is his season average on-court point differential, a read on how well his lineups are playing.
